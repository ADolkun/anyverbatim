# Language-policy eval (E2/E3)

Run 2026-11-15, after the loop repair (see [loops-2026-11-15.md](loops-2026-11-15.md)). Aggregates only, on public
corpora; no transcript or reference text. The owner's recordings are not in yet, so this decision rests on public data
and is revisited when they are.

## What was compared

All hypotheses are Whisper large-v3 with the production temperature schedule, and none gets a language hint.

| hypothesis | decode | language |
|---|---|---|
| P0 `w-prod` | the whole file at once | detected once, from the first 30 s |
| `w-vad` | 30 s speech chunks | detected per chunk among all languages |
| P2 `w-vad-lid` | 30 s speech chunks | detected per chunk, restricted to zh and en |
| P3 `w-runs-lid` | neighbouring same-language chunks merged into runs, each run decoded whole | detected per chunk, restricted to zh and en |

| corpus | what it is | metric |
|---|---|---|
| AMI | 6 English meetings, single distant microphone, 3.1 h | WER |
| AliMeeting | 8 Mandarin meetings, far-field channel 1, 4.2 h | CER |
| ASCEND sessions | 4 long-form Mandarin/English code-switched conversations, 1.2 h | MER |
| ASCEND utterances | the same 1315 utterances decoded one by one, 0.9 h | MER |

Error rates use the `itn` view (quantities and spelled-out numbers mapped to digits on both sides). Intervals are
paired bootstrap 95% CIs over recordings (`decision`), as pre-registered.

## Language detection without a restriction

`w-vad` and P2 decode the same chunks; the only difference is whether detection may pick any language.

| corpus | `w-vad`: chunks per detected language | P2: chunks per detected language |
|---|---|---|
| AMI | en 352, **cy 51 (18 of 147 min)**, nn 1 | en 404 |
| AliMeeting | zh 652, ko 2 | zh 654 |
| ASCEND sessions | zh 137, en 18, ru 1 | zh 138, en 18 |

Unrestricted detection labelled 12% of the AMI speech Welsh, and `w-vad`'s AMI WER is 10.8 points above P2's. AMI is
English, so no run ever sent it a prompt, and P2 scored 28.01% on AMI in the first run too: the gap is the restriction,
not the prompt-echo fix. P0 has the same weakness once per file: it decodes AMI TS3003b as Nynorsk.

## Translation incidents

An incident is a reference line that is at least 70% one script while the hypothesis words timed inside it are at
least 70% the other. Rule 3 reads the share among such lines in recordings whose reference holds both scripts.

| hypothesis | ASCEND sessions | ASCEND utterances |
|---|---|---|
| P0 `w-prod` | 170 of 894 (19.0%) | 10 of 301 (3.3%) |
| `w-vad` | 98 of 894 (11.0%) | 8 of 301 (2.7%) |
| P2 `w-vad-lid` | 103 of 894 (11.5%) | 8 of 301 (2.7%) |
| P3 `w-runs-lid` | 102 of 894 (11.4%) | 8 of 301 (2.7%) |

The sessions count depends on word timing, since a line takes the hypothesis words timed inside it; the utterances
count does not.

## Pre-registered rules

| rule | result | outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 1. P3 ships by default | — | P3 |
| 2. P2 replaces P3 only if it is better on the mixed data with the CI excluding 0, and its meetings CI is at most +0.5 points | ASCEND sessions P2 − P3 = +0.15 [−1.44, +1.07] | **P3 stays** |
| 3. Plan the two-token prompt if the shipped policy translates more than 1% of mixed chunks (reference lines here) | 11.4% of session lines, 2.7% of utterances | **planned** as a follow-up |
| 4. Stop if the shipped policy is worse than P0 on any corpus with the CI excluding 0 | no corpus | continue |

## What ships

E2 measured P3 with detection restricted to the languages spoken. So a recording uses P3 (`w-runs-lid`) when
`[asr] languages` lists them, for example `["zh", "en"]`, and P0 (`w-prod`) otherwise. An explicit `[asr] engine` or
`--engine` still wins, and YouTube stays on `w-prod`.
